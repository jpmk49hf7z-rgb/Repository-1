## The decision, in one paragraph

**Done-for-you operations automation for Shopify DTC brands doing $2M–$20M/year.** We build and maintain the automated workflows sitting between their store, helpdesk, 3PL, and spreadsheets — from a fixed menu of eight workflows, on a standardized stack. Retainers run $1,500 / $2,500 / $3,500 per month, with a $1,250 pilot rate capped at the first four clients in exchange for case study rights. The reasoning is in `01-niche-and-offer.md` §3; the short version is that this niche can prove itself in two weeks rather than six months, which is the only way the Day-75 case study milestone is reachable.

## Why the offer is built to not depend on you personally

You sell; the Week 4 hire delivers. Every structural choice reflects that:

- **A fixed menu of eight workflows**, not bespoke consulting — the hire builds from templates
- **One standardized tool stack**, with declining prospects outside it as an explicit rule
- **Written scope boundaries**, so the hire can say no without escalating to you
- **A guarantee about output** (two workflows in 30 days), not about client revenue — you can promise it on a sales call without knowing how the build will go

## What Week 1 deliberately does not cover

The plan's own closing note is that the biggest risk is over-investing in setup and under-investing in outreach. These are later milestones and are intentionally absent here: LLC and bank account (Week 2), website (Week 3), hiring and SOPs (Week 4), outreach accounts and prospect lists (Phase 2). The prospect-list criteria are already implied by the ICP in `01-niche-and-offer.md` §1 and can be lifted directly when Phase 2 starts.

## Actions that need a human

Some of this plan cannot be executed by an AI assistant and needs you personally: registering the LLC, opening bank and payment accounts, signing contracts, moving money, and being the legal party to any client relationship. I can decide, draft, research, and produce the work product for every step; the signing and the spending are yours. Where a step requires a licensed professional — the attorney review in Week 2 — that is called out in the file rather than worked around.

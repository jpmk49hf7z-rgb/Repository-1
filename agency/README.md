## The decision

**Bottom-of-funnel page programs for B2B SaaS companies, seed through Series A, with a marketing team of zero to two.**

Every month we research, write, design, and build a batch of high-commercial-intent pages on the client's site: competitor comparisons, "alternatives to X", integration pages, use-case pages, migration pages, and the landing pages they point to. Retainers are $2,000 / $3,500 / $5,000 per month, with a $1,750 pilot rate capped at the first four clients in exchange for case study rights.

## Why this shape

Three constraints decided it, in this order.

**Delivery is an AI assistant, so the deliverable must be an artifact.** No credentialed access to client systems, no on-call response, no continuous presence. That rules out anything operational and rules in anything produced in batches and reviewed before it ships.

**The plan needs proof by Day 75.** A page targeting "[Competitor] alternatives" ranks in weeks because nobody contests those terms, and the demo requests it produces are directly attributable. General SEO content cannot prove itself inside 90 days; that milestone would have slipped and taken the Phase 3 outreach ramp with it.

**Web design was the right instinct, but not as a project business.** A $4k site build resells from zero every time. The same skill applied to an ongoing page program compounds — there is always another competitor, integration, or vertical — which is what makes it a retainer. Delivering *built, published pages* rather than Google Docs is also the wedge against every content agency in this market.

## What changed from the first version

The initial Week 1 assumed a human employee and selected ops automation for Shopify brands. That offer required holding client credentials, working inside live production systems, and repairing broken workflows on call. None of it survives AI delivery. Replaced entirely.

Pricing moved above the plan's $1,500–3,000 band, to $2,000–5,000. With near-zero marginal delivery cost, capacity is no longer the constraint — **your attention is**, and it scales with client count, not client size. Fewer, larger clients means the same revenue for half the selling and half the reviewing. Five retained clients clears $100k.

## What is required of you

Two things, and both are non-negotiable:

**Review every page before it publishes.** About 15 minutes each, roughly 18 hours a month at six clients. You are checking claims about the client's product and about their named competitors — the two categories where an error is expensive. `03-delivery-model.md` §3.

**Run the production sessions.** Nothing is produced in a week you skip; I don't run between sessions. Two standing blocks a week is what makes the volume commitments in the agreement real.

## Positions taken

Recorded here because they will each be tested by a prospect, and deciding them under pressure goes badly:

- **AI use is never denied.** Not volunteered unprompted, answered honestly when asked, and a client with contractual AI restrictions is declined rather than signed and hoped for. `03-delivery-model.md` §5.
- **No ranking, traffic, or pipeline guarantees.** The guarantee is about output — six pages in 30 days — because that is what we control.
- **Comparison pages state where the competitor genuinely wins.** It converts better, and the alternative is a page that gets screenshotted.
- **Volume caps at 20 pages a month.** A quality limit, not a capacity one. Refuse the client who wants 500 programmatic pages.

## Actions that need you personally

Registering the LLC, opening bank and payment accounts, signing agreements, holding client credentials, and moving money. I can decide, draft, research, and produce the work at every step; the signing and the spending are yours. The Week 2 attorney review needs a licensed professional and is flagged in the file rather than worked around.

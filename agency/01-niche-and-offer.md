# Week 1 Deliverable 1 — Niche & Offer

**Status:** Decided (revised for AI delivery) | **Milestone:** "Niche and offer defined (Day 7)"

## 1. The constraint that drives everything

The delivery capacity in this business is an AI assistant, not a human employee. That is not a smaller version of a human hire — it is a differently shaped one, and the offer has to be built around the actual shape:

**Strong:** producing researched, written, designed artifacts at volume; consistency across dozens of similar deliverables; reading a competitor's entire public web presence in minutes; writing and building real HTML/CSS pages; doing this at a marginal cost near zero.

**Cannot do:** hold credentials or log into client systems independently, run continuously between work sessions, respond to an alert at 2am, be on a call, sign anything, or vouch for a factual claim about a client's product that no source material supports.

The rule this produces: **the deliverable must be an artifact that gets reviewed before it goes anywhere, produced in batches, where volume and consistency are the value.** Anything requiring live access, on-call response, or continuous presence is out of scope for this business — permanently, not until we grow.

## 2. The niche

**B2B SaaS companies, seed through Series A, with a marketing team of zero to two people.**

Funded enough to have a budget. Small enough to have nobody who can execute. Under constant board pressure to show pipeline. They buy on speed and they buy from a person who shows up with something already built.

## 3. The service

**Bottom-of-funnel page programs — built and published, not handed over as documents.**

Every month we research, write, design, and build a batch of high-commercial-intent pages on the client's site: competitor comparison pages, "alternatives to X" pages, integration pages, use-case pages by industry or role, and the landing pages those point to.

Your instinct about web design was right, and this is what it becomes when it has to fit a retainer. Straight web design is a project business — $4k once, then you resell from zero. This is web design with a reason to recur: there is always another competitor to compare against, another integration to document, another vertical to address. The client's page inventory compounds, and compounding assets are what retainers are actually for.

## 4. Why this, over the alternatives

| | BOFU page programs | Ops automation | Straight web design | General SEO/blog content |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI can deliver it end to end | Yes | **No** — needs live system access and on-call repair | Yes | Yes |
| Naturally recurring | Yes | Yes | **No** — project-based | Yes |
| Time to provable result | 3–8 weeks | 2–4 weeks | n/a | 4–6 months |
| Result is attributable to revenue | Yes — demo requests per page | Partly | No | Weakly |
| Survives "why not just use ChatGPT?" | Yes — see §7 | n/a | Weakly | **No** |
| Free sample can be built from public info | Yes — see §9 | No | Partly | Partly |

Two of those rows decide it. **Attribution:** a comparison page that brought in eleven demo requests is a case study by Day 75; a blog traffic chart is not. **The free sample:** everything a compelling sample requires — their competitors, their positioning, the keywords they're missing — is publicly visible, which turns cold outreach into something structurally different from what a human agency can afford to do.

## 5. Why bottom-of-funnel specifically

Someone searching "Notion alternatives" or "Segment vs RudderStack" has a credit card in their hand. Someone searching "what is customer data infrastructure" does not. The BOFU keywords are also long-tail and thinly contested — most competitors haven't built these pages at all — so they rank in weeks rather than quarters.

This is the whole reason the plan's Day-75 case study milestone is reachable. A general SEO retainer cannot produce proof inside 90 days. This can.

## 6. The page menu

Productization comes from building the same page types repeatedly, from templates, with research swapped in.

1. **`[Competitor] alternatives`** — one page per meaningful competitor
2. **`[Client] vs [Competitor]`** — honest, specific, and willing to name where the competitor is genuinely better
3. **`[Integration] integration`** — one per tool in their integration directory
4. **`[Product] for [industry]`** — vertical use-case pages
5. **`[Product] for [role]`** — buyer-persona pages
6. **`Best [category] tools for [segment]`** — listicle pages that include the client honestly
7. **Migration pages** — "switching from X", aimed at the highest-intent searcher there is
8. **Supporting landing pages** — the conversion destination the above point to

A typical client has 40–80 legitimate pages available in this menu. That is two to five years of retainer, which is the point.

### On honesty in comparison pages

Comparison pages that misrepresent a competitor are both wrong and bad business — they get screenshotted, they draw legal complaints, and they destroy the trust the page was built to earn. Our pages state where the competitor genuinely wins. Counterintuitively this converts better, and it is the only version I will write. If a prospect wants pages that distort a competitor's pricing or features, decline the client.

## 7. Answering "why not just use ChatGPT ourselves?"

Every prospect asks this. The offer has to answer it structurally, not rhetorically.

- **They won't.** The founder has meant to write comparison pages for eight months. The constraint was never text generation.
- **A page is not a document.** We deliver researched, designed, built, published pages with schema markup and internal linking. Most content agencies hand over a Google Doc and call it done — that doc sits in a folder because someone still has to build the page. That gap is the wedge.
- **The program is the product.** Competitor mapping, keyword-to-page assignment, sequencing by intent, refreshing pages when a competitor changes pricing. That's strategy, not prompting.
- **Consistency at volume.** Twelve pages a month, every month, at the same quality, is an operational achievement no busy founder replicates in spare time.

Do not hide that AI is used in production — see `03-delivery-model.md` §5. Selling the output while dodging the question is a bad trade: it is dishonest, and it hands any prospect who finds out a reason to churn and tell people why.

## 8. Pricing

**A deliberate departure from the plan's $1,500–3,000 range.**

The plan assumed a human employee, which made delivery capacity the binding constraint. It isn't anymore — marginal delivery cost is near zero. **The binding constraint is now your attention: hours spent selling, and hours spent reviewing work before it ships.** Both scale with client *count*, not with client *size*.

So: fewer, larger clients. Same revenue, half the sales load, half the review load.

| | **Launch** | **Growth** (target) | **Scale** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price | $2,000/mo | $3,500/mo | $5,000/mo |
| Pages built and shipped | 6 / month | 12 / month | 20 / month |
| Competitive gap analysis | on request | quarterly refresh | monthly |
| Refresh of existing pages | — | quarterly | monthly |
| Conversion testing | — | — | included |
| Reporting | monthly | monthly | monthly + call |

**Onboarding:** $1,500 one-time — competitive gap analysis, a full page map ranked by intent, and the designed page templates the whole program is built from. Real work, real deliverable, and it funds the first month. Waived for pilots.

**Pilot (first 4 clients only):** $1,750/month, 60-day initial term, then standard Growth pricing, in exchange for a written case study, a testimonial, and two referral introductions. Cap it at four so the discount never becomes the price.

**Guarantee:** the first six pages live within 30 days of receiving their source materials, or that month is free. An output promise, which we control. **Never guarantee rankings, traffic, or pipeline** — those depend on their product, their market, and their pricing. An agency that guarantees rankings is either lying or about to lose a client.

### Why $3,500 is defensible

At Growth, twelve pages costs roughly $290 a page. A freelance writer charges $500–800 for a comparison page and hands back a document someone else still has to build. An agency charges $1,000–1,500 built. For a SaaS company with a $12,000 annual contract value, one closed deal from one page pays for three months of the retainer. Sell it against their pipeline, never against a word count.

### What actually limits how many clients you can hold

Roughly 15 minutes of your review per page. Six clients averaging 12 pages a month is about 18 hours a month of review — real, but compatible with selling full-time. That number, not my capacity, sets the ceiling. `03-delivery-model.md` covers how to keep it there.

## 9. What this niche unlocks for sales

Recorded here because it shaped the niche choice, not to jump ahead into Phase 2.

Everything needed to build a genuinely valuable free sample for a prospect is public: their site, their positioning, their competitors, and the comparison pages those competitors already rank for. So cold outreach can lead with a **real artifact** — a short gap analysis naming the specific pages their competitors have and they don't, plus one finished page built for them, free.

A human agency cannot do this. Two hours of a person's time per prospect at 40 prospects a month is the entire month. At near-zero marginal delivery cost, it's routine. That asymmetry is the single largest advantage this business has, and it only exists because the niche was chosen so the sample could be built from public information.

Practically, it also inverts the sales call: you are not pitching what you might do, you are walking them through work already sitting on their screen.

## 10. Scope boundaries

**Included:** competitive and keyword research, page mapping, writing, design, building, on-page SEO and schema, internal linking, publishing support, monthly reporting, and refreshing pages we built.

**Not included:** ongoing management of their CMS or hosting; anything requiring us to hold their credentials or act inside their systems on a standing basis; paid media; general blog or top-of-funnel content on a separate calendar; email marketing; social media; brand or logo design; custom application development; and any claim about their product we cannot support from materials they provided.

Nearly every retainer that goes bad goes bad on scope. These go in the agreement before the first invoice.

## 11. Success metric for the offer

The offer works if discovery calls close above 25% without discounting under the pilot rate, and if a Growth client costs under 5 hours of your time per month after onboarding. Low close rate means the prospect list or the sample is wrong. High hours means the source pack is thin — fix it at the client, not by absorbing the work.

**Five retained clients by Day 120 clears $100k.** The plan's target of "6–8 clients by Day 90" was sized for $1,500–2,500 retainers; at this price point the same revenue needs roughly half the clients, and every client you don't have to sell is a week of selling you don't have to do.

Spend the slack on two things: keeping the clients you land, and building the free samples that land the next ones.
